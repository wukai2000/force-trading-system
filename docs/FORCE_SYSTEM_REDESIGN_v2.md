## Force

A **Force** is a persistent economic pressure that acts on multiple agents and produces a characteristic sequence of state changes.

It is **not** a residual IR, a ticker basket, or a public dataset.

Returns are downstream. Markets may be efficient at pricing what is currently knowable. The opportunity, if any, is **temporal propagation** through physical constraints, contracts, inventories, construction times, and organizational inertia.

The object of discovery is:

```
Force → competing Force → observable footprints → temporal ordering
     → changing Force balance → economic consequence
     → financial transmission → market recognition → falsification
```

The security is the last representation of the Force, not its definition.

## Target zone

Too early: 20-year structural trends. Too late: news, HFT, immediate arbitrage. Target: a process already unfolding, footprints measurable, full financial implication not yet resolved.

## Force fingerprint

Direction, ordering, time scale (weeks–years, not ticks and not decades), persistence, feedback, reversal. A single observable is underidentified. Failure if the fingerprint exists only after the financial move, only in hindsight, or only as one series.

## Force state

A winning / B winning / neither / transition / reversal. The question is which pressure is winning the tug-of-war, not whether indicator X is positive.

## Archetypes (not Forces)

A scarcity vs capacity · B cost-decline vs demand expansion (FS-0001 class, do not reopen) · C capacity vs saturation · D credit vs balance-sheet · E capital allocation vs physical constraint · F substitution · G replacement cycle · H regulation vs adaptation.
